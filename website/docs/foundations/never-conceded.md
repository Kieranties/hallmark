# Never conceded

## What it is \{#definition}

**Five things are never conceded: no deadline, no shortage of actors and no scoping
argument reaches any of them.**

## Why it exists \{#why}

**A short absolute list is learnable by a team with no practice. A long list of *shoulds*
is ignored wholesale.** Five is chosen to be memorable rather than to be complete, and the
cost of that is accepted: plenty of things that matter are not on it, and they are
protected by being [conceded](../loop/skipping-and-concessions.md) in writing instead.

**Everything else in the practice bends under pressure by design.** A step that had
something to do and was passed over becomes a recorded, scoped, expiring liability, and
the work continues. That arrangement only holds if a small number of things cannot be
bent at all — otherwise the concession becomes a universal escape hatch and the practice
has no floor.

**Each of the five breaks something that cannot be repaired afterwards.** That is the test
for admission to the list, and it is why the list is not a ranking of importance.

## Rules \{#rules}

**None of the following may be conceded, under any circumstance.**

### 1 · Contract graph closure

Conceding it breaks a consumer who never consented to the risk. The failure lands
somewhere other than where the decision was made, which is precisely what no scoping
argument can contain. *What a contract is, and what closure means, belongs to* Claims and
evidence.

### 2 · Legal and contractual assurances

A model version can be named in a log; it cannot be fired, sued, or sign an assurance.
Where an assurance is given, [accountability](../parties/actors.md) has to land on a
human, and no delegation dilutes that.

### 3 · The record of a claim being removed

A claim disappearing silently is the practice's anchor failure running in reverse —
somebody relying on something that is no longer there, with nothing to point at. *The
mechanism belongs to* Claims and evidence; *that it may never be skipped is fixed here.*

### 4 · The Verifier is not the Worker

There is no item, no deadline and no shortage of actors that permits one actor to hold
both [roles](../parties/the-four-roles.md) on one item. What the act itself requires is on
[verification](../loop/verification.md). Where there is genuinely one actor, the role is
recorded as unheld rather than quietly filled — see
[working alone](../loop/working-alone.md).

### 5 · Promotion of a characterisation test to a specification

**A characterisation test states what the code currently does, bugs included.** It is a
safety net for changing that code, and it is never a claim.

**A specification states what the system should do.** It is a promise.

**Promotion is the act of turning the first into the second, by confirming that current
behaviour is intended behaviour.** It requires a source of intent from **outside the
code** — the original request, the stated outcome, the client conversation — because
intent is not present in source. An actor holding only the source may not promote,
whether it is a person or an agent.

Skip it and you sell your defects as features, with evidence behind them.

### What absolute means here

**An absolute is not scoped, does not expire, and is not delegable around.** A role may
only be delegated by somebody who holds it, which is what stops the five being routed
around by handing the act to somebody else.

## In detail \{#detail}

### What to do when one of them is in the way

The absolute does not move, so something else does. The work stops, the scope changes, or
the thing that makes the absolute unsatisfiable gets fixed. Where an actor is missing,
a fresh agent session is the cheapest second one available. Where the intent behind
existing behaviour cannot be found anywhere, the characterisation test stays a
characterisation test and nothing is claimed from it.

None of these are workarounds. Each is the honest answer to *we cannot do this properly
right now*, and each leaves the count of what has been claimed correct.

### Why the list stays short

Every addition makes the list less likely to be known in full, and a list nobody knows in
full protects nothing. Anything that would be a sixth entry is better served by being a
standard — a floor, enforced by a build where it can be, and conceded in writing where it
genuinely cannot be met.

The five are not ranked. Each is on the list because conceding it damages something that
cannot be repaired by noticing later.
