# Skipping and concessions

## What it is \{#definition}

**A concession is the only permitted route to ship a known compromise: recorded,
attributable, scoped, expiring, visible and counted.**

## Why it exists \{#why}

**You will skip steps, and three different things hide behind that.** Only one of them is
a problem, and telling them apart is what stops the practice being either dishonest or
unusable.

**A step you are permitted to skip is a step nobody does.** Permission removes the
record, and without a record the compromise is invisible — which is the entire failure
the control exists to end. The concession does not prevent the compromise made at six on
a Friday. It converts an invisible compromise into a tracked liability.

**Recording it has to be cheaper than hiding it, or nothing gets recorded.** That is why
a concession is an item rather than a process: raising one costs what capture costs, and
capture is free.

## Rules \{#rules}

### Skipping

**Skipping is never *allowed*.** There is no state at which a step becomes optional.

**Three things hide behind a skipped step.**

| | What happened | What it costs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Nothing to do** | The step was **trivially true** — the work reached almost nothing, so there was nothing to plan and nothing to design | **Nothing.** Nothing was skipped |
| **You went anyway** | The step was not true and the work moved on — no specification, no plan, nobody checked it | **A concession.** Recorded, scoped, expiring, and counted until it is cleared |
| **Nothing was recorded** | The item moved and no trail exists | Visible. An item reaching `Completed` with no trail is obviously one |

**The difference between the first two is how much the work reaches**, never what type
it is. See [touch](./touch.md). A chore that reaches everything is not trivial because it
is a chore.

### Concessions

**A concession is attributable to a named actor**, with a human accountable through
delegation.

**A concession is recorded as an item on [the door](../items/the-door.md).** The item that
took the concession names it. Counting open concessions is therefore a query, and expiry
is a date on the item.

**A concession is scoped** — to one claim, one release, one client, or one role and
subject. An unscoped concession is a standard quietly lowered.

**A concession expires**, and expiry is not closure. One that expires unclosed is an
overdue debt, still open and still counted.

**A concession is visible to whoever it affects**, including a client where the
compromise reaches them.

**A concession generates debt automatically.** Clearing it is work, and that work is an
item like any other.

**Nothing on the [never conceded](../foundations/never-conceded.md) list may be
conceded.** Those five are absolute, and no deadline, shortage of actors or scoping
argument reaches them.

## In detail \{#detail}

### Why the count is never a target

**Open concession count is the sharpest signal the practice has, and targeting it destroys
it.** Told to reduce the number, people stop *raising* concessions rather than stop
*needing* them — which restores exactly the invisibility the control exists to destroy.
The metric would eat the mechanism it measures.

It may summon attention. It may never be the thing anybody is asked to improve.

### The concession is one of four retreats with the same shape

Every retreat from a stated position is attributable, recorded, scoped, expiring where
applicable, visible and debt-generating. A concession retreats from a standard we said we
meet. A [retraction](./stopping.md) retreats from a promise about the future. Two more —
a claim removed after it existed, and an out-of-band release outside the line everybody
else runs on — belong with *Claims and evidence*, which is not written yet.

A team learns the pattern once and applies it four times, rather than learning four
controls.

### Where a concession is not the answer

Where a role cannot reach something it needs, acting anyway is a concession — but the
first move is to make the thing reachable, because the absence is a target somebody owes
rather than a fact of life. See
[what a role carries](../parties/what-a-role-carries.md).
