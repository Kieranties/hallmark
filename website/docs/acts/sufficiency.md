# Sufficiency

## What it is \{#definition}

**Sufficiency is the set of information an act requires, which must be both defined in
advance and accessible at the moment of acting.**

## Why it exists \{#why}

**Supplied at the moment of acting, a requirement is an input. Written in a document, it
is something people are asked to remember.** That is the whole difference, and it is the
difference between a practice that holds under pressure and one that is recited at
induction and never again.

**Defined without accessible is a set nobody can use; accessible without defined is
improvisation.** Both halves have to hold. A stated set that cannot be reached produces
an act performed on less than it needed, and a reachable pile with no stated shape
produces a different act every time.

**The set is decided by what is being done and what it is being done to — never by who is
doing it.** That is what makes eligibility a question about access rather than about the
kind of actor, and it is why the substrate an actor reads cannot be a later concern: it
decides who is eligible to act at all.

## Rules \{#rules}

**Every act requires access to a defined set of information.**

| Both must hold | |
| --- | --- |
| **Defined** | The set is stated in advance, not improvised each time |
| **Accessible** | The [actor](../parties/actors.md) can actually reach every item in it, at the moment of acting |

**What is in the set is decided by what is being done and what it is being done to.**
Never by who is doing it.

**Sufficiency is governed by *enough*, never by *least*.** Least privilege governs what
may be **done**; the two must not be confused. See
[what a role carries](../parties/what-a-role-carries.md).

**Where any of it lives is not the practice's concern.** How many places it comes from,
and how it is fetched, is an application matter. The practice requires only that the set
is defined and reachable.

**If something in the set cannot be reached, the act cannot be performed.** That is not a
rule being broken — it is a role that cannot be held, and the fix is to make the thing
reachable.

**Acting anyway is a [concession](../loop/skipping-and-concessions.md)**, scoped to the
role and subject rather than to the act, and expiring like any other.

**The set is a [standard](./the-standards-stack.md), and standards stack.** What one act
must produce is the same shape again — see [required outputs](./required-outputs.md).

## In detail \{#detail}

### An unreachable input is a finding, not an obstacle

The instinct when something cannot be reached is to proceed on what is available and
mention it afterwards. The practice asks for the opposite reading: the absence is an
**enablement target** — something somebody owes and nobody has built — and it belongs on
the list of things to build rather than on a list of risks to watch.

This is also what gives a [discipline's](../parties/disciplines.md) needs teeth. *The
tooling makes this hard* had no mechanism behind it; an unreachable sufficiency item has
one, because it makes the role unholdable and the count of times somebody proceeded
regardless is kept.

### Where the set comes from

No single source supplies it. Visibility over the delivery of change is one part — where
each item sits, what has been published and where — and that has no home today; building
one is real work rather than a detail. Equally inputs, and not obtainable from it: the
code and the change under review, the item and the discussion on it, and what earlier
review cycles found.

### Why this is not access control

The question is *can this actor reach what it needs?* — never *is it prevented from seeing
too much?* Two actors on one item need much the same reading; what differs is what each
may do. Reading sufficiency as a permission model inverts it, and the practical result is
an actor denied an input it needed in order to be safe.
