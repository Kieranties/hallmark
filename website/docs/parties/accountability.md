# Accountability

**Attribution is not accountability.** An [actor](./actors.md) is identifiable, which gives
*attribution* — it can always be said what acted. A human is accountable, which gives
*consequence*.

Where an agent holds a [role](./roles.md), accountability is **derivative**: it attaches to
the human who delegated. The chain may be zero-length, when a human acts directly.

> A model version can be named in a log. It cannot be fired, sued, or sign a contractual
> assurance.

## Delegation

**The act of authorising a given actor to hold a given role, for a given scope.** It is
recorded, because it is where accountability lands.

Two rules bound it:

- **Ceiling** — nobody may delegate a role they do not themselves hold. You cannot grant what
  you do not have.
- **Least privilege** — the actor receives what its role requires, and the resources it is
  given at the moment of delegation. Nothing further.

**Nothing is undelegable.** In principle the whole practice may run on agent actors. When a
delegation goes wrong the fault does not disappear — it relocates to the **scoping act**,
which is recorded and therefore reviewable. The delegating human is answerable for having
scoped it wrongly.
