# Escalation

## What it is \{#definition}

**Escalation is a Decider handing a decision it cannot make from the recorded facts to
another [Decider](./the-four-roles.md).**

## Why it exists \{#why}

**A decision the facts do not support is not improved by being made sooner.** Where a
Decider is short of what the decision needs, the honest moves are to get the facts or to
hand the decision on. Neither is a failure of the role; deciding anyway is.

**The question does not get easier — it gets someone else.** That is the whole content
of the word here, and stating it prevents the two readings ordinary usage brings: that
escalating summons a more senior person, and that it turns a decision into an
emergency. Neither is true.

**Only one role can escalate, and the exclusion is the useful half.** A
[Verifier](./the-four-roles.md) that cannot settle the question from the evidence has
found that the evidence is insufficient. Letting it escalate would turn *nothing proves
this claim* into somebody else's judgement call, when producing sufficient evidence is
the Worker's job and always was.

## Rules \{#rules}

**Escalation belongs to the Decider, and to no other role.**

**A Verifier does not escalate.** It confirms, or it sends the work back — the two
outcomes and where the work returns to are on
[verification](../loop/verification.md#rules) and
[`Verified`](../loop/verified.md#rules).

**Escalation is a handoff within the same role.** It is never a change of role, and
never a severity ladder.

**The receiving Decider may be a person or an agent**, depending on who holds the role
there.

**With one actor there is nobody to hand it to. There are two moves and no third.**

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **The work stops** | Usually the right one. A decision the facts do not support is not improved by being made sooner |
| **The Decider decides anyway** | That is a [concession](../loop/skipping-and-concessions.md) — recorded, scoped, expiring and counted. Something has knowingly been decided on less than the decision required |

## In detail \{#detail}

### At one actor, escalating and conceding are the same move

They are one act seen from either side: *I cannot settle this* and *I settled it
anyway*. Reading **hand it to another Decider** as an instruction to go and find one, in
a repository with a single actor, produces a search that cannot succeed and an item that
sits still while it runs.

### What escalation is not

| It is not | Because |
| --- | --- |
| **A severity ladder** | Nothing about the question changes as it moves. There is no tier at which it becomes easier |
| **A route around an absolute** | A Decider may only delegate a role it holds, so nothing that is never conceded becomes conceded by being handed on |
| **A way to resolve insufficient evidence** | That is a failed verification, and it returns to whoever owes the evidence |
| **An escape from the record** | The receiving Decider decides from recorded facts too. Handing on a decision does not hand on permission to decide from memory |
