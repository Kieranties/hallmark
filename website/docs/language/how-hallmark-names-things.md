# How Hallmark names things

## What it is \{#definition}

**Hallmark reserves the words it uses: a reserved word carries one meaning inside the practice,
a word caught carrying two is retired rather than clarified, and every reserved word has
exactly one page that owns it.**

## Why it exists \{#why}

**A word with two readings lets two people agree while meaning different things, and neither of
them finds out until the work is done.** That is not a communication problem to be solved by
talking more carefully. It is a defect in the vocabulary, and it reproduces every time the word
is used.

**The practice is read by agents as well as by people.** An [actor](../parties/actors.md)
fetching a rule and finding a word that means two things has to decide which one applies, and
deciding is exactly what a definition exists to remove. A term that has to be interpreted is a
term that will be interpreted differently twice.

**Clarifying an ambiguous word does not work, and the reason is structural.** The clarification
lives in one place; the word travels everywhere. Six months later somebody who was not in the
room uses the word in its other sense, correctly by ordinary English, and the ambiguity is back
with nothing to point at. Retiring the word removes the thing that carried the ambiguity, and
leaves a record saying why.

## Rules \{#rules}

### Reserved words

**A reserved word means one thing within the practice, and only that.** Ordinary usage of the
same word outside the practice is irrelevant to what it means inside it.

**Every reserved word has exactly one owner page.** That page carries its definition. Every
other page that uses the term links to it and does not restate it — one fact, one home.

**A reserved term links to its owner page on first use.** A term used without its definition
reachable is a term the reader supplies from memory, which is the failure the reserved word
existed to prevent.

**Every reserved word is listed in [Reserved words](./reserved-words.md), with the page that
owns it.** An index entry points; it never defines.

### Retirement

**A word caught carrying two readings is retired on the spot** and replaced by words that each
carry one.

**Retirement is not clarification.** The word is not kept with a better explanation attached to
it. It is removed from the practice's vocabulary, and the readings it was carrying are given
separate names.

**A retired word is recorded in [Retired words](./retired-words.md)**, with the ambiguity it
carried and what replaced it. A word removed without a record is a word somebody reintroduces.

**A retired word is not used in the practice's own text**, and one appearing in an item, a
decision record or a page is a defect to fix rather than a style preference.

**Retirement is permanent.** A retired word is not brought back with a narrower meaning. Where
a new distinction is needed, a new word is minted.

### Naming

**A name must not smuggle in a claim the practice does not make.** The criterion applied to
[the four roles](../parties/the-four-roles.md) is the general one: a role name must not imply
which kind of actor holds it.

**Where a word survives with a discipline attached, the discipline is stated as a standing
habit rather than as new vocabulary.** Naming the object of an act — *the change*, *the
deployment*, *the feature* — is cheaper than minting three words for one act pointed at three
things.

## In detail \{#detail}

### The three shapes retirement takes

Most retirements are one shape. Two are not, and both are recorded so the pattern is not
mistaken for the rule.

| Shape | What triggered it |
|---|---|
| **Two readings, two remedies** | The ordinary case. One word named two things that behave differently, so it became two words |
| **Too weak rather than ambiguous** | One word named the right thing and made it sound optional. It was retired for what it implied, not for what it meant |
| **Caught before use** | One word was retired mid-adoption, before anything depended on it. Retiring early costs a line in a table; retiring late costs every document the word reached |

### Why the index is hand-authored

[Reserved words](./reserved-words.md) is written by hand rather than generated from page
metadata. Generating it would keep it honest automatically, and a build-time generator for a
list this size is apparatus with no consumer behind it. The cost is that the index can drift
from the pages, and the answer to that is a check that compares the two — not a generator that
replaces one of them.

:::note[The site is subject to its own rule]
These pages are the practice, so they are written to the discipline they describe. A page here
that defines a term another page owns is the same defect as a repository with two copies of a
standard.
:::
