# Reserved words

## What it is \{#definition}

**This page indexes every word the practice reserves and names the single page that owns each
one.**

## Why it exists \{#why}

**A vocabulary you have to already know is not a vocabulary.** An [actor](../parties/actors.md)
arriving at the practice — a person on their first week, an agent on its first fetch — needs
one place that turns a word into the page defining it, without first knowing which section that
word lives in.

**It is also the check on the rule it serves.** Every reserved word has exactly one owner page,
and the only way to see that two pages have started defining one term is to have the terms in a
list with their owners beside them.

## Rules \{#rules}

**Every entry is a pointer, not a definition.** An entry that explains a term has given that
fact a second home, which is the drift this index exists to make visible.

**Every reserved word appears exactly once**, against exactly one owner page.

**A word not listed here is not reserved**, and carries its ordinary meaning.

## In detail \{#detail}

:::note[The index covers the practice as written]
Sections 7 and 8 of the practice — claims and evidence, and applications — are not written yet,
so their terms are not indexed. The words below are the ones the site owns today. Terms named
on a page but owned by an unwritten one are listed at the foot.
:::

### Named here, owned by a page not yet written

These are reserved words the practice uses, whose owner pages belong to *Claims and evidence* —
the truth spine, and section 7 of the practice. They are named so that finding no entry above
is not read as *the word is free*.

**Capability catalogue · certified set · platform edition · edition registry · certification
record · contract · contract graph closure · withdrawal · deprecation · coverage · delivered ·
release · rollout · hotfix · health metric · product telemetry · process telemetry · version ·
build identity.**

The last two are used by [Commitment](../items/commitment.md), which promises work for a named
version and computes slippage against one. What a version *is* belongs with release material,
and that is written with *Claims and evidence*.
